\section{The certified 141/107 component separation}
Throughout the DNA atlas and weighted-component statements in this section, the parameter domain is exactly $\mathcal W$, with fixed labels and $A<C<G<T$ ties. The complete atlas does not classify other labelled antisymmetric weight maps.

\subsection{The complete fixed-family order-eight atlas}
The selector reduction of \Cref{ext:thm:two} makes the exact chamber classification finite. The wall-locality and mixed-wall formulas are \Cref{thm:regular-wall,thm:mixed-wall}; their technical arguments and the small-order regression are retained in the supplement (\Cref{sec:finite-atlas,sec:walls-atlas}). The component coverage is \Cref{prop:family-components}. We now give the complete fixed-$\mathcal W$ atlas, followed directly by its component optima and the separate 107 construction.

The alphabet is $A<C<G<T$ with weights $(-\rho,-1,1,\rho)$,
$\rho>1$. Zero-PCR ties are fixed: the smallest RC-fixed word in
a stable PCR, and the smallest word in a paired union together
with its RC mate.

Here is an explicit finite reduction for the root list. Let $V=\{\pm
e_1,\pm e_2\}$ and $D=V+V$, a nine-element alphabet. For a lower
word of seven letters write
\[
 x_1=v_0+v_6,\quad x_2=v_1+v_5,\quad
 x_3=v_2+v_4,\quad z=v_3.
\]
Its vector midpoint form satisfies
\[
 2r=\sqrt2(x_1-x_3)-2(x_1+x_2+x_3+2z).
\]
Enumerating $D^3\times V$ is exhaustive and positionally reversible.
For lambda use the same word, with $d_i$ the three paired differences:
$\lambda=(d_1+\sqrt2d_2+d_3)/\sqrt2$.
An affine scalar form $(a+c\sqrt2)\rho+b+d\sqrt2$ has, when its
slope is nonzero, the unique root
\[
 \rho=\frac{-ab+2cd+(bc-ad)\sqrt2}{a^2-2c^2}.
\]
Activity must be tested on one and the same positional word; independently
choosing pair sums and pair differences would be an invalid relaxation.

\begin{theorem}[Complete DNA terminal atlas]\label{thm:complete-atlas}
At $k=8$ there are exactly 86 distinct terminal walls in $\rho>1$:
87 open intervals and 86 point strata. They give 114 different
labelled terminal sets. All 86 walls separate different adjacent
terminals; at 27 walls the point is a third terminal, and at the
other 59 it equals one side. Each terminal has 8230 words.
The minimum number of a fixed dictionary of homogeneous linear
forms whose ternary signs determine the terminal is exactly 86.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Exact certificates and real-parameter reduction]
The reduction above enumerates every candidate; the exact comparison
and activity filters produce 86 midpoint roots, including all ten
lambda roots. The certificate stores each root and a realizing
word. On each open interval all signs are fixed, and structural
ties are fixed, so \Cref{ext:thm:two} proves constancy for every real
ratio, including transcendental ratios. Rational representatives
are chosen by exact dyadic bisection; point roots are evaluated
in $\Q(\sqrt2)$.

The producer and a separate verifier compare all membership bits;
the latter reconstructs the original seed in $\Q[X]/\Phi_8$.
Integer levels certify acyclicity, and explicit paths certify the
reported longest lengths. PCR and RC checks are literal. Terminals
are grouped by equality of bit vectors, not by assuming hash
injectivity. A separating word at each wall proves necessity.
A nonzero homogeneous linear form has at most one root on the
normalized line, so at least one form per indispensable wall is
necessary. Conversely one midpoint form at each of the 86 roots
distinguishes all strata. The files and replay commands are listed
in \pathfile{evidence/extended/README.md}.
\end{proof}
The minimum 86 concerns a fixed linear sign dictionary. It is not a
lower bound for adaptive queries, arbitrary nonlinear encodings,
or graph isomorphism classes.

\subsection{The optimum over the weighted component union}
\label{sec:weighted-component-union}
The complete atlas and \Cref{prop:family-components} supply 21
ordinary components at $k=8$. These are only the components
encountered by $\mathcal W$, with fixed labels and ties.
The next result optimizes their complete union, rather than only the
terminals of $\mathcal W$. \Cref{thm:construction-107} then separates this union
from the wider RC MDS space.

\begin{proposition}[Exact optima in the 21 components]
\label{prop:component-optima}
In each row of \Cref{tab:component-optima}, the ordinary optimum
and both RC-sector optima equal the displayed value. Hence the
minimum over the union of these entire components encountered by $\mathcal W$ is 141.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Exact certificates]
For each component, a simple cycle with one selected arc gives
the lower bound throughout that component. Two explicit MDS,
one per RC sector, attain it. The checker verifies the clock
inequalities, mate sums, gradients, Boolean membership, every PCR,
RC, ranks, paths and cycle intersections. A separate sink-removal
dynamic program confirms each length. The 42 sector solutions
are also reconstructed from ordinary clock solutions by tight-SCC
rounding. The proof does not enumerate each entire component.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Sharp parity cost and a shorter DNA construction}
\label{sec:construction-107}
The atlas of $\mathcal W$ is a starting family within the space of MDS. Other labelled antisymmetric weight maps are not classified here.
We now give finite witnesses outside it. All lengths in this and the
following sections count residual arcs in $B(q,k-1)$.

\begin{proposition}[The one-unit sector cost is attained]
\label{prop:sharp-parity}
For DNA at $k=8$ there is an RC MDS $M_*$ whose ordinary component
optimum is 124, while the optima in $\mathcal C_0(M_*)$ and
$\mathcal C_1(M_*)$ are respectively 125 and 124.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof with finite witnesses]
The directory \pathfile{evidence/advances/r1/witnesses/} supplies
\pathfile{Mstar125.words.txt} and \pathfile{M124_sector1.words.txt},
each an RC set of 8230 words.
Direct DAG reconstruction gives lengths 125 and 124. Their difference
is the integer gradient stored in
\pathfile{r1/certificates/retiming_Mstar125_to_M124.json}, whose mate
sum is odd, so they are in the indicated sectors of one component.

The parity certificate supplies a residual path $P:A^7\leadsto T^7$
of length 71 and a return path $Q$ of length 54 containing exactly
one selected arc of $M_*$. Their concatenation is a simple cycle
of length 125 with one selected arc. It gives the ordinary lower
bound 124. At period $T=125$, the two clock inequalities force
\[
 H(T^7)-H(A^7)=71.
\]
Sector zero requires $H(T^7)+H(A^7)=124$, hence
$2H(A^7)=53$, impossible for an integer clock. The length-125
endpoint attains the next possible value, whereas the other endpoint
attains 124. The independent verifier checks all arc differences,
both paths, the cycle and both DAGs.
\end{proof}
This resolves sharpness for a prescribed sector. It does not show that
the union of the two RC sectors ever has a worse optimum than the
ordinary component.

\begin{theorem}[Certified DNA upper bound 107]
\label{thm:construction-107}
There is an RC DNA MDS $M_{107}$ of cardinality 8230 with
$L(M_{107})=107$. Its ordinary component has exact optimum 107.
Consequently the global RC MDS optimum at $k=8$ is at most 107.
The upper-graph residual has length 106; a longest avoiding DNA
word has 114 letters, and every DNA word of length 115 contains
a selected eight-letter window.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Exact certificates]
The canonical witness is \pathfile{r5/witnesses/M107.words.txt}.
Its sorted ASCII lines, including the final newline, have SHA-256
\begin{center}\ttfamily\small
4e0c44d87ea9782f261cf3ed2c0e918784fa404bbf0f57041b494509ff7d5edc.
\end{center}
The checker enumerates all 65,536 arcs and 16,384 vertices, verifies
RC and one selection in each of the 8230 PCRs, and checks a rank
in $[0,107]$ increasing along each residual arc. A residual path
of 107 arcs attains the bound. A simple cycle of 108 arcs has
exactly one selected arc, proving the component lower bound by
\Cref{thm:unit-retiming}. Both path and cycle are included in
\pathfile{r5/certificates/M107.json}. The length conversions follow
from overlapping consecutive eight-letter words.
\end{proof}

Earlier component-optimal endpoints and their one-hit cycles are retained
in the companion; the first explicit escape is described in
\Cref{sec:construction-131}. A four-witness panel at length 107 certifies
two ordinary components with both parity sectors represented in each.
The complete stable fibre in \Cref{prop:stable-fibre} supplies further
components. Neither finite panel exhausts the global component space.

The 108-cycle forces any improvement to $L\le106$ to meet that
cycle at least twice, hence to leave this ordinary component.
Changing the intersection with one old critical cycle is only a
necessary condition: \Cref{prop:local-exclusions} includes examples
where all old critical cycles are repaired but the result is cyclic
or belongs to a component with a worse optimum.
